Dr Barran Dodger — an Australian whistleblower, researcher, writer and human-rights advocate — presents a public documentary archive of testimony, records, evidence, writing and the search for accountability.

01 · The Witness

A Life Preserved in Testimony

My name is Dr Richard William McLean. I write and publish under the name Barran Dodger, also known as Barran Resonance Dodger.

The Witness Remains is the public record I have built to preserve my testimony, writings, government correspondence, official records, administrative and legal material, forensic material, chronology and other documents accumulated across decades.

It exists because records matter.

It exists because memory alone can be challenged, misunderstood or lost. A preserved document can at least allow another person to inspect what was actually written, recorded, received or published.

02 · Purpose

Why This Archive Exists

This project is intended as a public finding aid for people who may wish to examine the underlying documentary record.

PRESERVATION

Preserve documents and records that may otherwise become difficult to locate or reconstruct.

PROVENANCE

Identify where material came from and, where available, preserve filenames, paths, hashes, timestamps and repository references.

ACCESS

Make publicly available material easier for journalists, lawyers, researchers and members of the public to locate.

EXAMINATION

Allow questions to be examined against the underlying record rather than relying exclusively upon assertion.

03 · Method

Preserve First. Examine Second. Conclude Last.

That is the central methodological principle of this archive.

PRESERVE FIRST.
EXAMINE SECOND.
CONCLUDE LAST.

The archive does not ask readers to accept every proposition merely because it appears in my testimony. Instead, the intention is to place testimony beside documents, dates, correspondence, responses and other identifiable material so that distinctions can be made.

04 · Evidence Classification

Not Everything Has the Same Evidentiary Status

The archive uses a simple classification framework to prevent different kinds of statements from being treated as though they have identical evidentiary weight.

A — DOCUMENTED FACT

A proposition directly supported by an identifiable record.

B — CORROBORATED CLAIM

A claim supported by additional identifiable material.

C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION

An allegation that is preserved in a documentary record but has not thereby been established as true.

D — INFERENCE / INTERPRETATION

A conclusion or interpretation drawn from available information.

E — SPECULATION

A proposition for which sufficient supporting evidence has not been established.

Some matters may additionally be identified as disputed, unresolved, author testimony, faith or spiritual belief.

05 · The Documentary Chain

Follow the Record

Where possible, the archive seeks to establish a documentary chain rather than presenting isolated conclusions.

06 · Government and Institutional Records

The Official Record Matters

The archive contains material relating to government correspondence, administrative processes, official records, legal matters, complaints, responses and other institutional interactions.

An official document is important because it identifies what an institution recorded, communicated or decided. It is not automatically proof that every proposition contained within it is correct.

Official records can themselves contain disagreement, error, incomplete information or competing accounts. Those distinctions should remain visible.

07 · Preservation

Digital Integrity

The project uses digital preservation methods including file provenance, SHA-256 hashing, repository history and, where available, blockchain or timestamp records.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A cryptographic hash can demonstrate that digital content corresponds to a particular hash value. Blockchain anchoring can assist with provenance and preservation. Neither, by itself, proves that the substantive claims contained in a document are true.

08 · Testimony

My Account Remains My Account

I have recorded events as I experienced, remembered, understood and documented them.

That testimony is important to the archive. But testimony is not silently converted into independently established fact merely because I have preserved it.

Where another source supports an account, that support should be identified. Where an account is disputed, that should be identified. Where the archive cannot presently establish the matter, it should remain unresolved.

12 · The Boundary

What This Archive Does Not Claim

NOT A COURT.
This archive is not a court or tribunal.

NOT A VERDICT.
The archive does not determine criminal guilt or legal liability.

NOT EVERY CLAIM IS ESTABLISHED.
Allegations, testimony, inference, speculation and faith remain distinguishable from documented findings.

NOT LEGAL ADVICE.
The archive does not replace advice or representation from a qualified legal professional.

NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR INDEPENDENT INVESTIGATION.
Where serious questions remain, an appropriately authorised independent examination may be necessary.

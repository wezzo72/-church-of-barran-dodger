CALLS &
RECORDINGS

A documentary finding aid for telephone calls, audio recordings, transcripts and other preserved voice communications relevant to the archive.

Purpose

Telephone calls and recordings can preserve contemporaneous evidence of conversations, requests, responses, statements and events that may not appear in later written records.

This section provides a structure for preserving and cataloguing those materials without treating an audio recording as proof of every proposition expressed within it.

A RECORDING PRESERVES A CONVERSATION

The existence of a recording may establish that a particular recording exists and, subject to authentication, may preserve what was said. Interpretation and truth of the statements remain separate questions.

Authenticity

Audio evidence should be examined with attention to provenance, continuity, file metadata, recording circumstances and whether the recording has been edited or transformed.

Provenance

Where did it come from?

Identify the original device, platform, export or source where known.

Integrity

Has it changed?

Preserve the original file and record any subsequent processing.

Continuity

Is it complete?

Identify gaps, edits, missing sections or uncertain continuity.

Identification

Who is speaking?

Identification should be based on appropriate evidence rather than assumption.

Transcripts

A transcript can make an audio record easier to search and examine, but it should remain connected to the original recording.

Where uncertainty exists in transcription, it should be marked rather than silently resolved.

TRANSCRIPT ≠ ORIGINAL RECORDING

The transcript is an interpretive representation of the audio. The recording remains the primary source where it is available and usable.

Legal and Privacy Considerations

Recording and publishing communications can raise legal and privacy questions that vary according to jurisdiction and circumstances.

The archive should therefore distinguish between preservation for evidentiary purposes and public publication.

Personal information belonging to third parties should not be unnecessarily exposed merely because it appears within a preserved recording or transcript.

Calls as Testimony

A call may preserve a contemporaneous account, request, warning, denial, response or other communication.

Its evidentiary significance depends upon authenticity, context, completeness and corroboration.

Evidence Classification

A

Documented Fact

The existence and provenance of the recording are directly documented.

B

Corroborated Claim

A proposition expressed in the recording is supported by independent material.

C

Documented Allegation

The recording preserves an allegation made during the conversation.

D

Inference

Meaning or causation inferred from the recording and surrounding evidence.

E

Speculation

A proposition not sufficiently established by the available material.

The Boundary

NOT A COURT · NOT A VERDICT

This page is a documentary finding aid. It does not establish the truth of statements made during any call or recording.

It does not determine criminal guilt, civil liability, misconduct, retaliation or human-rights violations.

Where a recording is disputed, incomplete, unavailable or unauthenticated, that status should remain visible.

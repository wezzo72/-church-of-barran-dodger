JUSTICE
MANDATE

A stated mandate for preservation, independent examination, accountability, human dignity and the lawful pursuit of justice.

01 · THE MANDATE

Why Accountability Matters

The Justice Mandate sets out the author's stated purpose in preserving and presenting the documentary record.

It is not a declaration that every allegation has been proven. It is a declaration that questions of potential wrongdoing, institutional conduct, human dignity and accountability should be capable of being examined against the available evidence.

THE PRINCIPLE

Serious questions should be answered by evidence, proper process and appropriately independent examination — not by assumption, silence or predetermined conclusions.

02 · CORE PRINCIPLES

The Mandate in Seven Principles

01 · PRESERVE

Preserve the documentary record before attempting to interpret it.

02 · IDENTIFY

Identify sources, dates, documents, institutions and provenance.

03 · DISTINGUISH

Separate fact, allegation, testimony, inference, speculation and spiritual belief.

04 · TEST

Compare assertions against primary documents and corroborating material where available.

05 · RESPOND

Record institutional responses rather than presenting only one side of a disputed matter.

06 · EXAMINE

Seek appropriately independent review where the circumstances warrant it.

07 · REMEMBER

Preserve the record for future journalists, researchers, advocates, lawyers, historians and members of the public.

04 · HUMAN RIGHTS

Human Rights as a Framework for Examination

The archive identifies international human-rights instruments as possible frameworks for examining the material. This does not constitute a finding that any particular right has been breached.

ICCPR

Civil and political rights, equality, liberty, privacy, expression and access to justice.

ICESCR

Economic, social and cultural rights and conditions relevant to human dignity.

CRPD

Disability equality, legal capacity, access to justice, freedom from exploitation and abuse, independent living and adequate living conditions.

CAT

The prohibition of torture and other relevant forms of cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment.

CEDAW

Equality and protection from discrimination against women, where relevant to the facts under examination.

CERD

Equality and protection from racial discrimination, where relevant.

CRC

Rights and protections concerning children, where relevant to the material being examined.

The relevance and legal application of each instrument depends upon the particular facts, jurisdiction and applicable law.

07 · INDEPENDENCE

The Call for Independent Examination

The author's position is that serious unresolved questions should, where appropriate, be considered by an independent authority with access to the underlying evidence and lawful investigative powers.

An independent investigation is different from an author's conclusion. Its purpose would be to test evidence, hear competing accounts, obtain relevant records and reach findings according to the applicable standard.

THE ARCHIVE DOES NOT PRETEND TO BE THAT INVESTIGATION.

It preserves material that may assist such an examination.

08 · ACCOUNTABILITY CHAIN

From Complaint to Resolution

ISSUE

A concern or alleged event is identified.

COMPLAINT

The concern is communicated to an appropriate body.

RESPONSE

The institution records its response.

INVESTIGATION

Evidence may be independently examined where authorised.

FINDING

A competent authority reaches a determination where empowered to do so.

REMEDY

Any appropriate remedy follows from the relevant finding and law.

09 · UNRESOLVED MATTERS

Uncertainty Must Remain Visible

The archive does not regard an unanswered question as proof of the author's preferred explanation.

Nor does the absence of a finding necessarily answer the underlying factual question.

Where the available record cannot establish an issue, the appropriate status may simply be:

UNRESOLVED.

That is not a failure of the archive. It is a commitment not to manufacture certainty where the evidence does not provide it.

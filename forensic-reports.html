Purpose

This page provides the master index for forensic material identified within the archive as Forensic Reports 01–10.

Each report should ultimately point to its original source document, filename, repository location and available provenance information.

FORENSIC DOES NOT MEAN INFALLIBLE

A report may provide analysis, methodology, technical observations or interpretation. Its conclusions should remain attributed to the report's author and tested against the underlying material.

Forensic Material May Include

DIGITAL

File Integrity

SHA-256 hashes, file metadata, Git history and other digital preservation material.

DOCUMENTARY

Document Analysis

Examination of documents, chronology, provenance and relationships between records.

TECHNICAL

Technical Analysis

Technical observations or analysis attributed to the relevant source.

CORROBORATION

Cross-Checking

Comparison between independent records where suitable material exists.

Forensic Chain

ORIGINAL SOURCE FILE METADATA HASH ANALYSIS METHOD RESULT LIMITATION REVIEW

A reliable forensic record should make it possible for another researcher to understand what material was examined, what method was used, what was found and what limitations apply.

Technical Preservation

Where forensic reports concern digital evidence, preservation information may include:

SHA-256

Cryptographic file fingerprint used for integrity and change detection.

Git

Repository history can preserve versions, commits and file paths.

Timestamps

Dates and timestamps can contribute to chronology and provenance.

Blockchain

Blockchain anchoring may provide an additional preservation or timestamping layer where independently verifiable.

INTEGRITY ≠ TRUTH

A technically intact document can still contain an inaccurate statement. Digital preservation establishes characteristics of the record, not automatically the truth of every proposition within it.

How to Read a Forensic Report

WHO? WHAT? WHEN? WHICH SOURCE? WHAT METHOD? WHAT RESULT? WHAT LIMITATIONS?

Researchers should distinguish an observation from an interpretation and an interpretation from a conclusion.

Where a report makes a substantive allegation, that allegation should be assessed separately against the underlying evidence.

Evidence Classification

A

Documented Fact

Directly established by an identifiable primary record.

B

Corroborated Claim

Supported by additional independent material.

C

Documented Allegation

An allegation preserved in a report or other record.

D

Inference

An interpretation derived from available material.

E

Speculation

A proposition not sufficiently established by available evidence.

Independent Examination

The archive's purpose is preservation and access. It does not replace independent forensic examination.

Where a matter is contested or consequential, an appropriately qualified independent expert may need access to the original material and complete provenance information.

The Boundary

NOT A COURT · NOT A VERDICT

This page is an index and finding aid for material identified as Forensic Reports 01–10.

The page does not independently adopt the conclusions of any report, establish criminal conduct, establish civil liability, or determine the truth of disputed allegations.

Until an underlying report is located and verified, no substantive finding should be attributed to that report by this archive.

TECHNICAL RECORD · PROVENANCE · PRESERVATION
01 · Purpose

Preserve what can be examined.

Digital evidence can be difficult to evaluate when files are moved, renamed, altered, compressed, duplicated or separated from their original context. This section records the technical preservation framework used within The Witness Remains where such information is available.

The objective is not to claim that a technical mechanism proves the truth of a document's contents. Its purpose is to preserve identity, provenance and integrity so that the underlying material can be examined.

IMPORTANT: A hash, timestamp or blockchain record can provide evidence concerning a digital file or its preservation history. It does not, by itself, establish the truth of every statement contained within that file.

03 · Integrity

What does a hash actually establish?

A cryptographic hash is a fingerprint of digital content. If the contents of a file change, its hash will normally change as well. This makes hashes useful for comparing copies and identifying whether the content of a particular file has changed.

SHA-256 A SHA-256 value can identify the exact digital content associated with that hash. It does not independently establish who created the document, whether the document is truthful, or whether the events described in it actually occurred.
04 · Blockchain

Anchoring is not authentication of truth.

A blockchain transaction can provide a durable public record associated with a hash or other digital identifier. This may strengthen preservation and chronology, depending on how the record was created and what exactly was anchored.

The blockchain record should therefore be interpreted according to what it actually establishes. It should not be presented as independent proof of the substantive truth of the underlying document.

06 · Forensic Caution

Technical certainty has limits.

Technical preservation can answer some questions very well: whether two files have identical content, whether a particular hash corresponds to a file, or whether a transaction records an associated hash.

Other questions require substantive investigation: who created a document, whether an account is truthful, what motivated an action, whether an event occurred, or whether conduct was unlawful.

DO NOT OVERSTATE THE TECHNICAL RECORD.

The archive distinguishes technical facts about digital objects from conclusions about the people, events or institutions described by those objects.

08 · Boundary

Not a court. Not a verdict.

Forensic or technical material should be read within its documented scope. The existence of a forensic report does not automatically establish every conclusion a reader may draw from it.

Where an independent authoritative finding exists, it should be identified separately. Where the available material is incomplete, disputed or unresolved, that status should remain visible.

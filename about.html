5. The Archive Is Not the Author's Verdict

A central principle of this project is that preservation and conclusion are different activities.

PRESERVE FIRST.
EXAMINE SECOND.
CONCLUDE LAST.

The archive therefore distinguishes between documented fact, corroborated claim, documented allegation, inference, speculation, testimony, faith and unresolved matters.

CLAIM → SOURCE → DOCUMENT → RESPONSE → OUTCOME → STATUS

6. Evidence and Testimony

The archive contains first-person testimony. Testimony is important because it preserves what the author says was experienced, observed, remembered or understood.

But testimony is not automatically an independently established fact. Where possible, the archive seeks corroboration through contemporaneous documents, official records, correspondence, recordings, legal material and other sources.

A — Documented Fact

Directly supported by an authoritative or primary record, subject to scope.

B — Corroborated Claim

Supported by multiple relevant sources or records.

C — Documented Allegation

Recorded as an allegation without thereby establishing it as fact.

D — Inference

An interpretation or conclusion drawn from available material.

E — Speculation

A possibility lacking sufficient documentary support.

8. The Human Rights Dimension

The justice and accountability sections identify human-rights instruments and Australian accountability mechanisms as frameworks through which relevant questions may be examined.

These include the ICCPR, ICESCR, CRPD, CAT, CEDAW, CERD and CRC, together with relevant Australian legal and administrative frameworks.

References to these instruments do not constitute a finding that a particular right was violated. They identify areas that may warrant independent examination against the underlying facts and law.

9. Digital Preservation

The archive uses digital preservation practices including public repositories, file provenance, SHA-256 hashes and, where available, blockchain or timestamp records.

These mechanisms can help preserve and identify digital objects. They do not independently prove the truth of the substantive contents of those objects.

11. The Witness Remains

The title of this project is both documentary and personal.

It describes an attempt to ensure that a life cannot be reduced to an administrative file, a disputed description, a missing document, an isolated allegation, or someone else's version of events.

The answer is not to demand that every assertion be believed. The answer is to preserve the record and allow it to be examined.

A LIFE → A TESTIMONY → A BODY OF WRITING → AN ARCHIVE → A RECORD → A MINISTRY → HOPE.

12. The Invitation

Read the documents. Follow the sources. Check the chronology. Distinguish record from allegation. Distinguish testimony from finding. Distinguish faith from evidence. Preserve uncertainty where uncertainty remains.

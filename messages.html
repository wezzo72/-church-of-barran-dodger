MESSAGES

A documentary finding aid for written communications, correspondence, messages and other preserved communications relevant to the archive.

Purpose

Messages can provide contemporaneous evidence of what people said, requested, reported, knew or communicated at a particular time.

This section is intended to catalogue and preserve relevant written communications without silently converting communications into findings about what actually occurred.

A MESSAGE RECORDS A COMMUNICATION

A message may establish that a communication was made. It does not automatically establish the truth of every statement contained within that communication.

Authenticity and Preservation

Digital communications can contain important metadata, including dates, timestamps, sender and recipient information, attachment information and platform-specific identifiers.

Screenshots can be useful preservation records but may not contain all available metadata. Where possible, the original digital record should also be retained.

ORIGINAL EXPORT FILE HASH ARCHIVE PUBLIC REFERENCE

SHA-256 or other integrity mechanisms can help demonstrate that a particular preserved digital file has not changed after hashing. They do not independently prove the truth of the communication.

Privacy and Redaction

Communications may contain personal information belonging to the author, correspondents or unrelated third parties.

Public presentation should therefore distinguish between preservation of an underlying record and publication of every piece of information within that record.

PRESERVE WITHOUT NEEDLESS EXPOSURE

Sensitive personal information should not be published merely because it appears in a preserved communication. Where appropriate, records can be retained while public copies are redacted.

Evidence Classification

A

Documented Fact

The record establishes that the communication exists or occurred.

B

Corroborated Claim

The communication's relevant proposition is supported elsewhere.

C

Documented Allegation

The message records an allegation made by a correspondent.

D

Inference

Meaning attributed to a communication beyond its literal contents.

E

Speculation

A proposition not adequately established by the available records.

Messages and Testimony

Messages may form an important part of first-person testimony because they can preserve a person's contemporaneous account rather than a later recollection.

Nevertheless, contemporaneous does not automatically mean accurate. Communications should be examined alongside independent records, chronology and responses.

The Boundary

NOT A COURT · NOT A VERDICT

A message may document that something was said or communicated. It does not automatically establish the truth of the proposition communicated.

Allegations contained within messages remain allegations unless independently established.

The archive preserves communications so that they can be examined in context, compared with other records and assessed fairly.

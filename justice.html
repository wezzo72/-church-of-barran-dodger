The Witness Remains · Accountability

JUSTICE

A public-interest framework for examining records, accountability, institutional responsibility, human dignity and the search for independent review.

Justice Is a Question, Not a Verdict

The Witness Remains does not claim to be a court. Its purpose is to preserve material that may assist journalists, lawyers, researchers, advocates, investigators, public officials and other readers in examining questions of accountability.

The archive distinguishes between what a document establishes, what a person alleges, what can be corroborated and what remains unresolved.

NOT A COURT. NOT A VERDICT.

The archive does not determine criminal guilt or legal liability. It preserves and organises material so that questions can be examined by people and institutions with appropriate authority.

The Justice Mandate

The author's stated purpose is not simply to preserve a personal history, but to seek accountability, dignity and an independent examination of the documentary record.

01

TRUTH

Preserve the underlying record rather than allowing important documents to disappear from view.

02

ACCOUNTABILITY

Ask appropriate institutions to examine documented conduct and unresolved questions within their lawful jurisdiction.

03

DIGNITY

Treat the people described in records as human beings whose rights and dignity matter.

04

INDEPENDENCE

Seek examination by bodies capable of considering evidence without relying solely upon the account of one interested party.

05

TRANSPARENCY

Make source material and provenance visible wherever lawful and practicable.

06

REMEDY

Preserve the question of appropriate remedy where harm is established by a competent authority.

Questions for Independent Examination

WHAT HAPPENED?

What events are supported by contemporaneous records?

WHO KNEW?

What information was available to relevant decision-makers at the relevant time?

WHAT WAS DONE?

What actions, decisions or omissions are documented?

WHAT WAS THE RESPONSE?

How did institutions respond when concerns were raised?

WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

Which questions cannot presently be answered from the available material?

WHAT SHOULD BE REVIEWED?

Which matters warrant examination by an appropriately independent authority?

Human Rights Context

The archive identifies possible human-rights questions for consideration in the appropriate legal and institutional context. These references are not presented as findings that a particular right has been breached.

ICCPR

Civil and political rights, including questions of equality, liberty, privacy, expression and access to justice.

ICESCR

Economic, social and cultural rights and the conditions necessary for human dignity.

CRPD

Disability rights and equal participation, including the principles reflected in Articles 5, 12, 13, 16, 17, 19, 22, 25 and 28.

OTHER CONVENTIONS

The archive identifies the possible relevance of CAT, CEDAW, CERD and CRC depending upon the facts and legal questions under examination.

International-law references require legal analysis of the particular facts, jurisdiction and applicable domestic law. They are not themselves findings of breach.

Australian Accountability Context

The archive may contain material relevant to questions involving Australian government departments, agencies, service systems, administrative bodies and public authorities.

Depending upon the subject matter and jurisdiction, relevant avenues may include courts and tribunals, complaints bodies, ombudsman processes, integrity bodies, human-rights mechanisms, disability-rights mechanisms or other authorised review processes.

AUTHORITY MATTERS.

The existence of an allegation or documentary record does not determine which institution has jurisdiction to investigate it. Each matter must be assessed according to the applicable law and procedure.

Evidence Before Conclusion

The justice section depends upon the same evidence discipline used throughout the archive.

A — DOCUMENTED FACT

Directly supported by an identifiable record.

B — CORROBORATED CLAIM

Supported by additional identifiable material.

C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION

Recorded allegation without an archive finding of truth.

D — INFERENCE

An interpretation drawn from available information.

E — SPECULATION

A proposition not sufficiently established by available evidence.

UNRESOLVED

No reliable conclusion presently established.

What the Archive Seeks

The stated objective is independent examination rather than predetermined condemnation.

Where records establish wrongdoing, the appropriate response should be determined by competent authorities. Where allegations cannot be established, that should also be recorded honestly. Where questions remain unresolved, the uncertainty should remain visible.

Preserve the record. Ask the question. Let the evidence be examined.

Justice and Hope

Justice within this archive is not presented only as punishment. It also includes dignity, restoration, protection, truth, accountability and the possibility of a future beyond the circumstances being documented.

This connects the documentary purpose of The Witness Remains with its spiritual themes of mercy, courage, love and hope.

The Boundary

NOT A COURT. NOT A VERDICT.

The Witness Remains is a documentary archive and first-person research project. It does not determine criminal guilt, establish legal liability, replace legal advice or claim to exercise the powers of a court, tribunal, regulator or investigative authority.

Allegations remain allegations unless established by an appropriate authority or supported to the relevant evidentiary standard.

The search for justice begins with preserving what can be examined.

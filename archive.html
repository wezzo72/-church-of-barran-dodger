04 · Archive Method

Preserve first. Examine second. Conclude last.

The archive is designed to help readers move from proposition to source rather than from conclusion to assumption.

CLAIM → SOURCE → DOCUMENT → RESPONSE → OUTCOME → STATUS

Where material is disputed, alleged, unresolved or interpretive, the distinction should remain visible.

05 · Status Language

What does the record actually establish?

DOCUMENTED

Documented

Supported directly by an identifiable record, subject to the scope and reliability of that record.

CORROBORATED

Corroborated

Supported by multiple relevant records or sources.

ALLEGED

Alleged

A proposition recorded as an allegation rather than established as fact.

DISPUTED

Disputed

The proposition is contested or contradicted and should not be presented as settled.

UNRESOLVED

Unresolved

Available material does not presently permit a responsible conclusion.

NO FINDING LOCATED

No Finding Located

No authoritative determination has been located in the material examined so far.

06 · Independence

Read it yourself.

The archive does not ask the reader to accept every interpretation made by its author. Its strongest proposition is simpler: preserve the underlying record so that other people can examine it.

Journalists, lawyers, researchers, historians, investigators, advocates, academics, public officials and members of the public may approach the material from their own professional or analytical perspective.

This website is a documentary finding aid. It is not a court, verdict, criminal finding, legal determination or substitute for independent investigation or legal advice.

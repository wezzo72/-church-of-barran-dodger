Purpose of This Register

This page is intended to provide a structured index for matters that the author describes as crimes or potentially criminal conduct.

The word “crimes” in this page title reflects the author's description and does not itself constitute a legal finding that a crime occurred.

IMPORTANT STATUS NOTICE

An entry in this register is not, by itself, proof that a crime occurred. Each matter must be assessed against its underlying evidence, applicable law, jurisdiction and evidentiary standard.

Where no competent authority has established criminal guilt, the matter should remain identified as an allegation, claim, inference or unresolved issue as appropriate.

How to Read the Register

A — DOCUMENTED FACT

Directly supported by an identifiable record.

B — CORROBORATED CLAIM

Supported by additional identifiable material.

C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION

An allegation preserved in a record but not thereby established as true.

D — INFERENCE

An interpretation drawn from available information.

E — SPECULATION

A proposition not sufficiently established by available evidence.

UNRESOLVED

The available material does not presently establish a reliable conclusion.

A classification describes the evidentiary status of the material, not a finding of criminal guilt.

Documentary Standard

Before an entry is populated, the preferred documentary chain is:

1 · MATTER

What is alleged to have occurred?

2 · DATE

When is the event said to have occurred?

3 · SOURCE

What is the original source?

4 · DOCUMENT

What identifiable record exists?

5 · RESPONSE

What response was received?

6 · OUTCOME

Was there a determination or other outcome?

7 · STATUS

What can presently be established?

What Counts as a Primary Record?

Depending upon the matter, primary material may include correspondence, official records, legal documents, administrative records, complaints, responses, recordings, messages, contemporaneous notes, published material, forensic material or other identifiable source documents.

The preferred practice is to link directly to the original public source wherever possible.

A summary or AI-generated description should never silently replace the underlying document.

Responses and Counter-Evidence

A credible finding aid should preserve not only material supporting an allegation but also material that contradicts, disputes or qualifies it.

Where a person, organisation or institution has provided a response, the response should be identified and preserved where lawful.

NO SILENT CONVERSION

An allegation does not become a fact merely because it is repeated. Repetition is not independent corroboration.

Criminality Requires Legal Determination

Whether conduct constitutes a criminal offence depends upon the applicable law, the elements of the offence, evidence, jurisdiction, procedure and the relevant standard of proof.

This archive cannot make that determination on behalf of a court or other competent authority.

Accordingly, this page should be understood as an index of matters raised by the author and/or documented in the underlying archive, pending examination of the primary material.

What Testimony Means Here

Testimony is the first-person record of a person's experience and account of events. It can be deeply important without automatically being the same thing as independently established fact.

This archive therefore preserves testimony alongside documentary evidence, official records, legal material, correspondence, forensic material and other sources wherever available.

TESTIMONY IS PRESERVED — NOT AUTOMATICALLY ADJUDICATED.

The author's account is presented as the author's account unless an independent source establishes something further. Readers are encouraged to examine the underlying records.

The Witness

Dr Richard William McLean, who writes and publishes as Barran Dodger, has described his life, work, experiences, writings and encounters with institutions through a substantial body of first-person material.

The Witness Remains brings that testimony into one public documentary framework so that personal accounts can be considered alongside the documentary record rather than existing only as isolated statements.

The purpose of preservation is not to force the reader to believe. It is to make the record available for examination.

Evidence Classification

The archive uses a distinction between what is documented, what is claimed, what is alleged and what remains interpretive.

A — DOCUMENTED FACT

A proposition directly supported by an identifiable record.

B — CORROBORATED CLAIM

A claim supported by additional identifiable material.

C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION

An allegation recorded in documentary material but not established as fact by the archive.

D — INFERENCE / INTERPRETATION

A conclusion or interpretation drawn from available material.

E — SPECULATION

A possibility or proposition for which sufficient evidence has not been established.

UNRESOLVED

The available record does not permit a reliable conclusion.

The Boundary

NOT A COURT. NOT A VERDICT.

This testimony archive does not determine criminal guilt, establish legal liability, replace legal advice or independently establish every allegation contained within the material.

Its purpose is preservation, transparency, contextualisation and examination.

A witness can preserve a record. A reader can examine it. An independent investigator can test it. A court can determine matters within its jurisdiction. These functions should not be confused.

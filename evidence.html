01 · Purpose

The record comes first.

The Evidence section of The Witness Remains is intended as a finding aid for the underlying documentary archive. Its purpose is preservation, provenance, navigation and critical examination — not the manufacture of conclusions.

The archive contains material described by Barran Dodger as including government correspondence, official records, legal and administrative documents, FOI material, messages, recordings, chronology, forensic material, writings and records of digital preservation.

IMPORTANT: A document being present in an archive does not, by itself, prove every proposition that someone may draw from it. Documents must be read in context, authenticated where appropriate, compared with other records, and distinguished from interpretation.
02 · Evidence Chain

Follow the chain.

Where possible, an allegation or proposition should be traceable through the underlying record rather than presented as an unsupported conclusion.

03 · Classification

Not everything in a record is the same kind of evidence.

The following working classification is intended to make the distinction visible to readers.

A · DOCUMENTED FACT A proposition directly established by an identifiable primary record or official source, subject to the limits of that record.
B · CORROBORATED CLAIM A claim supported by more than one relevant source or independent record, without implying a judicial finding.
C · DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION An allegation recorded in a document, correspondence, complaint, statement or other identifiable source.
D · INFERENCE An interpretation or conclusion drawn from available material rather than directly established by the underlying record.
E · SPECULATION A possibility, hypothesis or proposition for which sufficient documentary support has not been established.
UNRESOLVED
Some matters may remain unresolved. “Unresolved” is not a finding that something did or did not occur. It records the present state of the available material.
04 · Preservation

Preservation is part of the evidence.

The archive seeks to preserve source material in a form that allows future readers to locate the underlying record and examine its provenance.

01

Original source

The preferred reference is the original public document or repository location wherever available.

02

Filename & path

Original filenames and repository paths can provide useful provenance and navigation information.

03

Git history

Public repository history may provide additional information about when a file was committed or preserved.

04

SHA-256 & hashes

Cryptographic hashes can be used to identify a particular digital file and detect subsequent changes to its contents.

05

Blockchain records

Where blockchain or timestamp records are provided, they should be treated as preservation or provenance records rather than automatic proof of the truth of the underlying document.

06

Independent examination

Preservation allows journalists, lawyers, researchers, historians, investigators and other qualified reviewers to examine the material independently.

05 · Source Integrity

Do not confuse preservation with proof.

A hash can demonstrate that a particular digital object has a particular content state. A blockchain timestamp can establish that a record was anchored at a particular point in time. Neither mechanism, by itself, determines whether the underlying assertions contained in a document are true.

Likewise, the existence of an official government document can establish that the document exists and may establish what an agency recorded, decided, communicated or received. It does not automatically establish that every assertion inside that document is correct.

THE RULE Preserve the source. Identify the provenance. Read the whole record. Compare related documents. Identify the response. Record what remains unresolved.

07 · A Boundary

Not a court. Not a verdict.

The Witness Remains is a documentary archive and finding aid. It does not determine criminal guilt, civil liability or legal responsibility.

The presence of a document, allegation or testimony in the archive should not be interpreted as an independent finding that the allegation is true. Disputed matters remain disputed or unresolved unless supported by an appropriate authoritative determination.

Nothing on this page replaces legal advice, professional investigation, judicial determination or independent review.
